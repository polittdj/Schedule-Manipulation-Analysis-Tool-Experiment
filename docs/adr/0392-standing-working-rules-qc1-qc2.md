# ADR-0392 — The two standing working rules: QC-1 (prove or refute before you report) and QC-2 (read everything, verify everything)

Status: accepted (2026-08-13). Standing operator directive — binding on every session, no exceptions.

## Context

`CLAUDE.md` has always carried **two non-negotiable laws** about the *product*: data sovereignty
(Law 1) and fidelity over speed (Law 2). It carried nothing of equivalent standing about the
*method* — how a claim is permitted to become an action.

What it did carry was a sentence: *"READ EVERYTHING, ASSUME NOTHING, VERIFY EVERYTHING"* — buried
as a trailing clause at the end of the ADR-0240 model-protocol section. Its placement predicted its
fate. It was routinely skipped, because a rule that is a footnote inside another rule is read as
commentary on that rule rather than as an obligation.

The 2026-08-13 full-repo audit made the cost concrete, and the failures were all one shape — **a
claim was allowed to become an action without an executable check that could have refuted it**:

- The engine's ADR-0108 defect was carried, unfixed, through two reverted attempts because both
  implemented the ADR's *description* of the mechanism rather than re-deriving it. The description
  was wrong: the mechanism was never the data date (ADR-0391).
- A first cut of that same fix reproduced the fixture's expected date and looked proven. It was
  circular — `tools/make_test_projects.py` writes those dates with the very rule under test. Only
  an operator challenge caught it before it landed.
- The Law-1 locality guarantee turned out to rest on one unpinned frozenset. Widening
  `net_guard._LOOPBACK_HOSTNAMES` with a hostname left **226** guard/air-gap/startup tests green,
  reproduced at **854**. The guard suites pinned sampled negatives and never the set itself.
- A blast-radius sweep globbed `**/*.xml` and silently missed every `.mspdi.xml.gz` golden —
  including the primary 2,126-activity reference, where the change's largest effect lived. The
  sweep looked exhaustive and was partial by construction.

Each of these is cheap to catch with a check that is *able to fail*, and expensive to catch any
other way. The operator therefore directed that both obligations become first-class, mandatory
rules.

## Decision

Add a new `CLAUDE.md` section, **"The two non-negotiable working rules"**, placed immediately after
the two product laws and carrying the same standing:

**QC-1 — Prove or refute it before you report it.** Before any change is made, before any
conclusion is drawn, and before any document is updated: construct an executable pass/fail check,
run it in a sandbox, and use it to try to REFUTE the claim — before the result is reported. With
five sub-obligations: red before green (the check must be *observed to fail*); executable beats
inspectional; sandbox and never mutate the instrument; prove the check has teeth by mutation; and
when a check is genuinely impossible, mark the claim UNVERIFIED in the deliverable rather than
assert it silently. Plus the independence requirement: a fixture generated by the rule under test
cannot validate that rule.

**QC-2 — Read everything, verify everything.** Read everything, skip nothing, assume nothing,
verify the validity of everything — documentation, code, comments, tests, configuration,
instructions, prior sessions' claims, and `CLAUDE.md` itself. **If any error is found, QC-1 applies
before the correction is made** — the fix is itself a claim. With four sub-obligations drawn from
measured failures: inherited claims are testimony, not evidence; know a number's provenance before
trusting it; a claim derived from configuration describes intent, not behaviour; and scope a
finding precisely before acting on it.

The buried ADR-0240 sentence is **promoted, not duplicated** — that paragraph now points at QC-2,
and states that ADR-0240's "no finding is reported until the lead has independently re-verified it"
is QC-1 applied to multi-agent work.

**The product laws keep their numbering.** Laws 1 and 2 are cited in **110 source and test files**
and in five other documents as "the two non-negotiable laws". Renumbering to four would have
invalidated all of it for no gain, and the two kinds of rule are genuinely different: the laws
constrain the artifact, the working rules constrain the method.

Naming: **QC-1/QC-2**, verified collision-free across the tree, and coherent with the existing
`qc-checker` subagent. *Falsification* was the natural term and was rejected on evidence —
`falsify` / `falsified` / `falsifying` are **banned accusatory terms** in `ai/citations.py`'s figure
gate, and TP4's planted manipulation is literally a "falsified baseline". The word means something
else here.

## The rules were applied to their own creation

This ADR would be self-refuting otherwise.

1. `tests/test_standing_rules.py` was written **first**, against a `CLAUDE.md` that did not yet
   contain the rules, and **observed to fail**: 3 substantive assertions red, 2 controls green —
   so the failure was real rather than vacuous.
2. The rules were then written, and the guard went green.
3. A **mutation battery** was run — and **caught a defect in the guard itself**. Two mutations
   escaped: deleting `sandbox`/`refute` from QC-1's binding sentence still passed, because both
   words survived in QC-1's bullet list; and softening "MUST be observed to FAIL" passed because
   the bare token `fail` still matched "never failed" further down. The clause checks were
   file-global, not scoped to each rule's own section — *a census can be exact and still not be
   membership*.
4. The guard was hardened (per-rule section slicing via `_rule_section`, and phrase-level rather
   than token-level pins) and the battery re-run: **12/12 mutations caught by name**, control
   green, `CLAUDE.md` md5-verified identical after every restore.

Mutations covered: delete either rule entirely · bury the section heading · comment a rule out ·
soften the mandatory language to advice · strip the sandbox/refute obligation · drop
red-before-green · drop the mutation obligation · drop the UNVERIFIED-disclosure obligation · drop
the QC-2 → QC-1 interlock · replace the red-before-green phrase with a synonym.

## Consequences

- Two obligations that were previously advisory (one of them a footnote) are now binding, and are
  regression-pinned like a security constant. This directly applies the audit's headline lesson:
  **a guard is only as strong as the test that pins its DATA**, and a standing rule is data.
- QC-1 imposes real cost on every task. That is the intent — the failures it prevents (a wrong
  parity number, a false sovereignty claim, a circular validation) are the expensive kind, and this
  project's own history supplies the examples.
- `tests/test_standing_rules.py` asserts **substance, not byte-exact prose**: wording around the
  binding clauses may be improved freely. A guard that fires on ordinary editing gets deleted, and
  then it guards nothing — the same reasoning `.githooks/pre-commit` uses for leaving prose alone.
- **Deliberately NOT done:** no hook enforces QC-1 mechanically. A hook cannot tell whether a check
  was capable of refuting its claim, and a mechanical proxy for that judgement would be theatre. The
  rule is enforced by the working discipline and pinned against silent deletion; that is the honest
  boundary of what automation can do here.
